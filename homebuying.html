What is Debt-to-Income Ratio?

Your DTI is the percentage of your gross (pre-tax) monthly income that goes toward debt payments.

DTI = Monthly Debt Payments ÷ Gross Monthly Income × 100

Example: If you earn $5,000/month and pay $1,800 in debt (car + student loan + credit cards), your DTI is 36%.

Why it matters: Most conventional loans require DTI under 43%. FHA allows up to 57%. The lower your DTI, the more house you can qualify for.

→ CFPB: Calculate your DTI (trusted calculator)

What is Escrow?

Escrow is an account your lender manages to collect and pay your property taxes and homeowner's insurance on your behalf.

How it works: Your monthly mortgage payment is split — part pays down principal and interest, part goes into your escrow account. When taxes and insurance are due, the lender pays them from that account.

Can you opt out? Sometimes — some lenders let you pay taxes and insurance separately if you put 20%+ down. But many require escrow. Always ask upfront.

Why it matters: If your tax assessment increases after you buy, your escrow payment goes up — and so does your monthly payment. Always verify the tax history before closing.
